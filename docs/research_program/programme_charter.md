# Programme charter — Inclusive organisational preparedness for lone-actor violence in educational settings

**Charter ID:** `RP-CHARTER-2026-07-31`  
**Status:** adopted for programme planning (not a completed study)  
**Paper 1 role:** empirical and methodological foundation — **not** a literature review substitute and **not** the final programme outcome

## Programme title

Inclusive Organisational Preparedness for Lone-Actor Violence in Educational Settings  
*(working short title: Inclusive School Preparedness Programme)*

## Societal problem

Educational centres — including inclusive mainstream schools and special education settings — face rare but severe acute threats, including lone-actor violence. Occupants have diverse functional needs. Doctrine, organisation, environment, detection, decision, and protective action must work together without treating disability as deficit or as a proxy for dangerousness.

## Scientific question

How can educational centres prevent, detect, decide, and respond to lone-actor violence and related acute threats in ways that ensure protective actions are executable for occupants with diverse functional needs, and how can that organisational capability be measured and improved?

## Theoretical problem

Literatures on demand–capability fit / TCI / HRA, protective-action decision making, access and functional needs, usable security, work-as-imagined vs work-as-done, behavioural threat assessment, and organisational preparedness remain fragmented. They have not been unified into a school-specific, ethically bounded model of **organisational preparedness** under adversarial acute threat. A critical error to avoid: treating threat detectability as demand–capability fit, or collapsing executability and detectability into a single “inclusive-security” score.

## Methodological cascade

1. **Doctrinal audit (Layer 1)** — what authoritative guidance engages with protective-action executability (Paper 1).  
2. **Instrument architecture** — translate doctrinal gaps and theory into measurable organisational domains (Paper 2 / SLAPA).  
3. **Field validation** — reliability and validity in centres (Paper 3).  
4. **Independent detectability module** — sensing / BTAM / false-positive safeguards (Paper 4; SLAPA Domain B).  
5. **Later pathway** — practice guidance, intervention studies, and cautious policy synthesis (post–Paper 3; funding-dependent).

## Four-paper structure (minimum coherent set)

| ID | Role | Status |
|---|---|---|
| **RP-P1** | Documentary audit of protective-action executability engagement | **active** |
| **RP-P2** | SLAPA architecture and content validation | **planned** |
| **RP-P3** | Field validation in inclusive and special education settings | **planned** |
| **RP-P4** | Threat detectability and false-positive safeguards (companion) | **planned** |

Do not merge RP-P1 and RP-P4. Do not expand the set without compelling methodological reason.

## Cumulative contribution

RP-P1 reduces uncertainty about **doctrinal under-specification**. RP-P2 converts that map into a multidimensional organisational instrument architecture. RP-P3 tests whether the architecture yields reliable, interpretable field measures. RP-P4 develops the theoretically independent detectability branch so that “more sensing” is never confused with “more executability.”

## Claims boundaries (programme-level)

- Documentary engagement ≠ organisational preparedness ≠ exercised performance ≠ incident outcome.  
- No single combined inclusive-security score.  
- Domain profiles only; no categorical ranking of school types as safer/less safe.  
- SLAPA is **not** validated until field validation criteria are met.  
- No claim that any instrument predicts or prevents attacks.

## Practical pathway

Usable assessment framework → centre-facing guidance (non-punitive) → intervention studies anchored to weak domains → evidence for European security/education policy and Horizon-facing synthesis (scientifically conservative).

## Five-year horizon

Realistic sequence under limited resources: finish RP-P1 → RP-P2 content work → RP-P3 field validation → optional RP-P4 / interventions / policy briefs. See `five_year_roadmap.md`. Guaranteed publication dates are not implied.

## Resource assumptions

Limited researcher capacity; incomplete comparator source access (CF-02) currently blocks complete documentary pilot; field studies require ethics approval and school access; Horizon or other external funding is desirable but not assumed.

## Programme-level risks

| Risk | Mitigation |
|---|---|
| Treating RP-P1 as final destination | Charter + operational priorities: Priority 1 remains RP-P1 execution |
| Scope creep into Paper 1 | Paper 1 spine fixed to executability engagement only |
| Detectability merged into demand–capability | Forbidden; Domain B / RP-P4 independent |
| Premature “validated instrument” claims | Future claims policy |
| Ranking school types | Comparison safeguards |
| Programme docs displacing operational work | Operational priority register |

## Explicit statement on Paper 1

**RP-P1 is the empirical foundation of the programme.** It is a focused documentary audit of executability engagement in authoritative guidance. It is not a preliminary literature review, not a field preparedness assessment, and not the final programme outcome.
